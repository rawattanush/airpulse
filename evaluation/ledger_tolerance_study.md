# Tolerance of the cross-platform ledger comparison: justification

Generated by `scripts/ledger_tolerance_study.py` on 2026-10-07 (Windows-11-10.0.26200-SP0, Python 3.12.10). Figures: `evaluation/ledger_tolerance_study.json`. No benchmark value was changed; the study writes nothing to the store, the ledger or the registers.

## Question

`scripts/check_benchmark_ledger.py` accepts a regenerated ledger as NUMERICALLY EQUIVALENT when, among other conditions, every probability of a fitted model is within a tolerance of the ledger of record. Does that tolerance only absorb rounding differences between platforms, or could it hide a change of the method?

## Answer

The rule: for each model the tolerance is the smallest power of ten that is at least 100 times the largest platform difference measured for that model. It is justified if it also lies at least 100 times below the smallest effect of a change of the implementation and if every such change is refused.

| Model | Quantity | Measured across platforms | Tolerance by the rule | Tolerance in the check | Tolerance over measured | Smallest effect of an implementation change | Smallest effect over tolerance | Justified |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Logistic model | largest difference in a row | 8.79e-13 | 1e-10 | 1e-10 | 114 | 4.54e-05 (features as 32-bit numbers instead of 64-bit) | 454,441 | yes |
| Logistic model | mean difference over all probabilities | 7.68e-15 | 1e-12 | 1e-12 | 130 | 6.01e-08 | 60,070 | yes |
| Boosted trees | largest difference in a row | 5.55e-17 | 1e-14 | 1e-14 | 180 | 1.21e-02 (101 trees instead of 100) | 1,212,754,767,257 | yes |
| Boosted trees | mean difference over all probabilities | 1.33e-19 | 1e-16 | 1e-16 | 751 | 1.49e-04 | 1,492,141,899,639 | yes |
| Three rule baselines | any probability | 0 | identical required | identical required | — | — | — | yes |

Every implementation change that alters a probability is refused (9 changes). Adversarial verification (section D): 43 of 43 deliberate alterations of the Linux ledger got the verdict that was designed and that first-principles arithmetic predicts.

**Verdict: the tolerances are justified by these measurements.** Across the two platforms no predicted class changes (0), no hit or miss changes (0), and the largest change of any reported metric at full precision is 1.45e-13.

What the first version got wrong. The check was first written with one tolerance of 1e-9 for both models, taken from the size of the logistic model's differences. This study showed that for the boosted trees that number was nine orders of magnitude above their platform difference and would have hidden a real change (dose-response table: a learning rate altered by one part in a billion). The tolerance is now set per model by the rule above; for the logistic model the rule gives 1e-9, for the boosted trees 1e-13. A tolerance on the mean difference was added by the same rule: with a row tolerance alone, many rows could each be moved by a little less than the tolerance and the ledger would still pass (section D).

What no tolerance can do. A tolerance cannot see a change whose effect on every probability is smaller than the tolerance. Section C states what such a change could alter at most, and the dose-response table shows how small it has to be. A change that alters no probability at all (listed in section B) gives the identical ledger; no comparison of outputs can see it. On the machine that produced the record no tolerance is used: the audit and the tests require the pinned checksum there, so a change of a single bit is seen.

## D. Adversarial verification on the known-good Linux ledger

The Linux ledger (`evaluation/platform_check/forecast_ledger_linux.csv`) was altered deliberately, one alteration per case, and each altered ledger was compared with the ledger of record. Three statements must agree for a case to count as expected: the verdict the case was designed to get; the verdict of an oracle that computes, without calling the comparison, the row difference, the mean difference and the change of the mean log loss and mean squared error in every group the altered rows belong to; and the verdict of the comparison. A rejected case must be rejected by every rule the oracle predicts, an accepted case must break no rule at all.

Probability difference: the largest difference of a probability of that forecaster between the altered ledger and the record. Metric difference: the largest change of any reported metric at full precision; n/a where a probability is not a number, because no metric is computed then. Expected is the designed verdict; the oracle's verdict equals it in every case.

Comparator semantics at exactly the tolerance. Documented in `scripts/check_benchmark_ledger.py` (`EQUALITY_PASSES`): a difference is refused only when it is greater than its tolerance; a difference exactly equal to the tolerance is accepted. Observed: Logistic model: equality accepted; Boosted trees: no stored value allows a difference exactly equal to its tolerance, so the nearest representable difference below it was accepted and the nearest above it refused.

Small changes tested together. Logistic model: two changes, each accepted alone, are refused together: the log loss of their group moves by 1.2 times the metric tolerance. Boosted trees: not constructible: within the row tolerance of 1e-14 no alteration moves a metric by 0.6 of the metric tolerance (smallest probability of a class that occurred: 9.53e-03); many rows together are refused by the mean tolerance instead.

What the cases establish.

- Genuine platform noise is accepted: the Linux ledger as built breaks no rule.
- The row tolerance is a boundary, and it is inclusive. Logistic model: a difference of 9.9999952763e-11 (the largest representable one not above 1e-10) is accepted; the next representable difference, 1.0000000827e-10, is refused. Boosted trees: a difference of 9.9920072216e-15 (the largest representable one not above 1e-14) is accepted; the next representable difference, 1.0047518373e-14, is refused.
- A difference equal to the tolerance to the last bit is accepted, and one representable step more is refused. Such a difference exists as a number only for very small stored values: Logistic model: 5 stored probabilities allow it (tested); Boosted trees: 0 stored probabilities allow it (the nearest representable differences are the boundary).
- A class or a hit that changes is refused whatever the probabilities are: in those cases no probability was touched, so no tolerance is involved.
- A baseline that changes by one unit in the last place is refused.
- A metric that moves by more than the metric tolerance is refused even when the probability moved far less than its row tolerance: for the logistic model a probability near zero raised by about 1e-12 moves the log loss of its group by 1.1 times the metric tolerance and is refused, and by 0.9 times and is accepted. For the boosted trees no alteration within the row tolerance can move a metric that far (their smallest probability for a class that occurred is about 0.009), so those cases are refused by the row tolerance first.
- Changes that are small one by one are refused together when together they move a metric beyond its tolerance: for the logistic model two alterations that are each accepted alone are refused when both are present.
- Many rows moved in the same direction, each by less than the row tolerance, are refused by the mean tolerance. The count in the table is the most rows that can each be moved by half the row tolerance before that happens; one more is refused.
- A probability that is not a number is refused also in a forecast that is not scored yet. The first version of the comparison would have ignored such a row; that was found while preparing these cases and corrected.

What the cases do not establish. An alteration that is accepted is, by these rules, of rounding size in every row and on average, and moves no reported quantity: one probability moved within its row tolerance, a handful of rows moved within the mean tolerance, every row moved by one unit in the last place. Such an alteration cannot be told from platform rounding by any comparison of two ledgers. On the machine of record it is still seen, because the checksum is required there.

## Limits of this justification

- One other platform was measured (Linux under WSL on the same processor). Another processor or another build of the numerical libraries can round differently. If its difference exceeds a tolerance the check fails with DIFFERENT, which is the intended result: it is then a finding to examine, not a number to widen.
- The changes in section B are examples. A change that was not tested may have a smaller effect.
- Package versions are pinned in `requirements.txt`. The study says nothing about other versions.
- The rule's factor of 100 is a choice. With a factor of 10 the logistic tolerance would be 1e-10 and would see hyperparameter changes ten times smaller, with less room for another platform's rounding.
