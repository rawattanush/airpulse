# Free Hosting Audit

Read at the provider on 2026-10-06. Generated by `scripts/build_hosting_docs.py`; do not edit.

The question: can AirPulse run for $0 a month, without a payment card, with the owner's computer switched off, from one repository? Nothing here is assumed from the words "free tier": price, card, limits and terms were read on the provider's own pages, and what could not be read is marked NOT READ.

## Answer

One provider is enough: a public repository on GitHub, GitHub Actions for everything that computes, GitHub Pages for the site. $0 a month, no card named by the provider for the free plan, no server, no database, no second service, no secret.

Three things limit it, and they are the provider's, not the design's: Pages may not be used for a site primarily directed at commercial transactions; a scheduled run can start late and is switched off after 60 days without repository activity; everything in a public repository is public.

## Why one repository, and why not the research repository

GitHub Pages on the free plan needs a public repository, and a public repository redistributes what it holds. The research repository holds, in its files and in its history, material whose redistribution is not established: the archive of headlines of two trade titles whose terms could not be read, the reference annotation set built on them, files of a publisher that forbids commercial use, aggregates of flight lists under a research licence, and copies of papers. So the hosted repository is a separate, production-only repository, assembled through an allow-list (`deploy/public/manifest.yaml`, `scripts/build_public_repo.py`): the engine at its root, the customer application in `airpulse-web/`, one workflow.

History is not carried over. It contains those files; an allow-list applied to one tree can be checked file by file, one applied to every past commit cannot be checked with the same certainty. The first commit names the two commits it was built from, and the engine's source is taken whole, so its code hash is that of the research commit. The research repositories stay as they are, with their history, as the scientific record. Sources switched off in the hosted repository: `news_aircargoweek`, `news_splash247`, `eurocontrol`, `flight_lists_2019_2022`.

## What would make it not $0

- A private repository together with Pages: needs a paid plan. Not used.
- Larger runners, or minutes of a private repository beyond the quota. Not used.
- A custom domain: the domain itself costs money at a registrar. Optional; the github.io address is free.
- A commercial flight-data, freight-rate or news licence: none is used; the features that would need one are not in the product.

## Terms the owner has to weigh

1. **GitHub Pages and commerce.** The provider does not allow Pages as free hosting for an online business or for a site primarily directed at commercial transactions or commercial software as a service. A public information site with attribution is the use this design makes of it. If AirPulse is ever sold, takes payments or sits behind a login, the site must move to hosting that allows it; the workflow would then upload the same `dist` folder elsewhere.
2. **GitHub Actions and the project.** Actions is for building, testing and publishing the software of its repository. The workflow does that, and fetches a few public files a day to do it. It is not a general compute service, and the provider may limit use it judges out of proportion.
3. **Everything is public.** Code, configuration, operational data and every past run are readable by anyone. No secret exists to leak, but the owner must want the code public. No licence to reuse it is granted unless the owner adds one.
4. **The fuel prices: an open point the owner decides.** The index is read at the Bureau of Labor Statistics (public domain). The fuel prices are read at the Energy Information Administration, whose reuse page allows use and distribution with an acknowledgment; its tables name a commercial data vendor as the source of the spot prices, and whether that limits reuse was not asked of the Administration (KI-107). The owner publishes them on his own decision, recorded in the production policy with its basis, and removes them on objection; no confirmation of the publisher is claimed. The relay services through which these series were read before (FRED and ALFRED) are retired: their terms forbid keeping the data and building models on them (KI-101).
5. **Schedules.** A run can start late, and at high load a queued run can be dropped. In a public repository schedules are switched off after 60 days without repository activity. The workflow commits the day's flight lists every day; whether the provider counts a commit made by its own token as activity is not stated in its documents (KI-091). If the schedule is ever switched off, it is switched on again with one click on the Actions tab, from any browser.
6. **Photographs and logo.** They come from the owner's brand kit and are published with the site. Their origin is not recorded in either repository; the right to publish them is the owner's to confirm (KI-092).
